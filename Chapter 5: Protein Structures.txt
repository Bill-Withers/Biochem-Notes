Over 25,000 genes in humans (Only 2% of genome)
Amino acid sequence defines shape, which defines function (Native fold has large number of favorable interactions within)
20-30% of human proteins are polymorphic

Favorable Interactions:
  -Hydrophobic effect
  -Hydrogen bonding
  -Van der Waals forces
  -Electrostatic interactions
    -Electrostatic interactions + hydrogen bonding = salt bridge

Phi = φ = angle of α-carbon-amide bond
Psi = ψ = angle of α-carbon-carbonyl bond
φ + ψ = 180 when fully extended

Protein Structure Levels:
  -1 = amino acid covalent bonds
  -2 = repeated backbone pattern (ex: α-helix)
  -3 = polypeptide chain
  -4 = assembly of multiple polypeptides

Sterics make certain angle combos unfavorable
Potential hydrogen bonds make certain angle combos more favorable
Ramachandran plots show φ + ψ distributions in a protein 

Trans resonance = No rotation on a peptide bond (6 atoms in a rigid plane)
Rotation on α-carbon

Secondary Structures:
  -Local spatial arrangement of polypeptide backbone
  -α-Helix:
    -Stabilized by hydrogen bonds between nearby residues
  -β-Sheet:
    -Hydrogen bonds between adjacent β-strands, may be not nearby
  -β-Turn:
    -Protein turns 180 degrees in 4 amino acids
  -Irregular Arrangement:
    -Random coil or loops

α-Helix:
  -N-H from backbone hydrogen bonds with C=O from four residues earlier (n to n+4)
  -3.6 residues per turn (5.4Å rise per turn = 1.5Å rise per amino acid)
  -Hydrogen bonds are roughly parallel with the helical axis
  -Side chains point out, roughly perpendicular to axis
  -R-chain interactions between n to n+3 and n to n+4
  -Small Hydrophobic amino acids like Leucine and Alanine are helix promoters
  -Proline is a helix breaker, rotation with α-carbon is impossible
  -Glycine is a helix breaker, too flexible
  -large dipole moment over entire structure
    -negative residues near positive dipole (Amino terminus)

β-Sheet:
  -β-strands are zig-zag shaped (pleated)
  -Hydrogen bonds between N-H and C=O of backbones in different strands 
  -Side chains alternate up and down
  -Antiparallel = Hydrogen bonds straight across, 7Å between peaks
  -Parallel = Hydrogen bonds angled across, 6.5Å between peaks

β-Turns:
  -Occur when strands in β-Sheets change direction
  -180 degrees over 4 amino acids
  -stabilized by n to n+3
  -Type I turn = Proline in position 2
  -Type II Turn = Glycine in position 3
  -Most peptide bonds, except Proline are trans
  -6% of Proline bonds are cis, most of these are in β-Turns
  -Proline Isomerase catalyzes Proline isomerization

Determining Secondary Structure:
  -Ramachandran Plot (in silico)
  -Circular Dichroism (in solution)

Circular Dichroism Analysis:
  -Asymmetric molecules have different absorptions in left-handed and right-handed circularly polarized light
  -Δε = εL - εR (Molar absorption difference)
  -Chromophores in the chiral environment produce characteristic signals
  -CD signals from peptide bonds depend on chain conformation]
  -See CD Analysis Picture for more info

Loops:
  -Too big to be highly structured turns
  -Not necessary flexible, but they can move
  -Variable in primary sequence

Tertiary Structures:
  -Overall spatial arrangement of atoms in a protein 
  -Stabilized by R-chain interactions: 
    -Hydrophobic effect and polar interactions 
    -Disulfide bonds
    -Long range interactions
  -Classes:
    -Fibrous:
      -Long strands or sheets
      -Single type of secondary structure
      -Insoluble, Hydrophobic
      -Structural role
    -Globular
      -Polypeptide chain folded into spherical or globular shape
      -Multiple types of secondary structures
      -Most enzymes and regulatory proteins

α-Keratin:
  -2 right-handed α-Helixes
    -Amphipathic (1 side polar, 1 side non-polar)
    -Rich in hydrophobic amino acids
    -5.2Å per turns
  -Left-handed supercoil
  -Disulfide bonds make Keratin harder

Collagen:
  -Long Glycine and Proline rich chain, unique secondary structure
  -left-handed helices (NOT ALPHA, 3 amino acids/turn)
  -3 helices make one right-handed triple super helix (3 lefts make a right)
  -Repeating Sequence: Gly-X-Y
    -X is often Proline
    -Y is often 4-hydroxyproline
  -Collegen molecules staggered and crosslinked
    -NOT Disulfide bonds
    -Bonds are between Lysine and 5-Hydroxylysine

4-Hydroxyproline in Collagen:
  -Forces proline into favorable pucker
  -Offers more hydrogen bonds 
  -Post-translational processing catalyzed by prolylhydroxylase
    -requires α-ketoglutarate, molecular oxygen, and ascorbate (vitamin C)
    -reaction oxidizes Fe2+ to Fe3+, vitamin C reduces it back to Fe2+

Silk Fibroin:
  -Antiparallel sheets
  -Small R-chains (Alanine and Glycine) allow close packing of sheets with interlocking R-groups
  -Stabilized by hydrogen bonds within sheets and LDF between sheets
  -Sheet structure is extended, silk does not stretch
  -Numerous weak interactions make it flexible

Water-Soluble Globular Proteins:
  -Segments of chain fold back on each other, providing structural diversity for various functions
  -Enzyme, transport protein, motor protein, regulatory protein, immunoglobulins, etc.
  -Hydrophobic residues on inside
  -Most polar R-groups on the surface
  -Very compact

X-Ray Crystallography:
  -Steps:
    -Purify proteins
    -Crystallize the protein
    -Collect diffraction data
    -Calculate electron density
    -Fit residues into density
  -Pros:
    -No size limits
    -Well-established
  -Cons
    -Difficult for membrane proteins
    -Cannot see hydrogens

Bimolecular NMR:
  -Steps:
    -Purify proteins
    -Dissolve the protein
    -Collect NMR data
    -Assign NMR signals
    -Calculate the structure
  -Pros:
    -No crystallization
    -Can see hydrogens
  -Cons
    -Difficult for insoluble proteins 
    -Works best with small proteins 

3-Dimensional Globular Proteins:
  -Motifs:
    -Super-secondary structure (NOT part of hierarchy)
    -Specific arrangement of several secondary structures:
      -All α-Helixes
      -All β-Sheets
      -Both
    -Examples
      -β-α-β loop (See Picture)
      -β-barrel (See Picture)
      -Coiled coil
      -Helix-turn-helix
      -Globin = arrangement of 8 α-Helices
  -Domain:
    -Polypeptide chain independently stable from protein
    -Independent folding in multiple domains 

Intrinsically Disordered Proteins:
  -Contains segments that lack a definable structure
  -Allows protein to interact with many proteins 
  -Lysine, Arginine, Glutamate, and Proline force less defined structure
  -p53 = "The Guardian of the Genome", Tumor Suppressant 

Proteostasis:
  -Maintenance of proteins
  -Steps:
    -Biogenesis
    -Folding
    -Trafficking
    -Degradation
  -Folding:
    -Function depends on shape
    -Denaturation = Loss of functional structure
    -Denatured by:
      -Heat or Cold
      -pH extremes
      -Organic solvents 
      -Chaotropic agents:
        -Urea
        -Guanidinium hydrochloride

Ribonuclease Refolding:
  -Ribonuclease A contains 8 Cysteines, making 4 disulfide bonds
  -Urea and 2-mercaptoethanol (together) fully denature it
  -Once they are removed, Ribonuclease A refolds on its own into its native conformation
  -Sequence defines native conformation

Chaperones:
  -Facilitate folding
  -Native Structure is most thermodynamically stable
  -Hsp70 (Heat Shock Protein) chaperonins prevent misfolding due to heat
  -See GroEL/GroES picture
