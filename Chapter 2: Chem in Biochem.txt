Weak Interactions:
  -Ionic:
    -Electrostatic interactions between permanently charged species, or between the ion and a permanent dipole
  -Dipole-Dipole:
    -Electrostatic interactions between uncharged, but polar molecules
  -Van der Waals:
    -Weak interactions between all atoms, regardless of polarity
    -Attractive (dispersion) forces depend on polarizability. Strong at "long" distances (4-7 Å)
    -Repulsive (steric) forces depend on atom size. Stronger at short distances
    -Universal force
  -Hydrophobic Effect:
    -Complex phenomenon associated with the ordering of water molecules around nonpolar substances
    -Non polar molecules stick together in aqueous solutions 
    -Favors ligand binding:
      -Drugs take advantage of this for delivery
  -Hydrogen Bonding:
    -Attraction between Hydrogen atoms attached to electronegative atoms and other highly electronegative atoms with a lone pair
    -Responsible for water's high melting and boiling point:
      -H-Bonding in water is cooperative, each bond makes the next one easier to form
    -Bond strength depends on direction: 
      -Inline is stronger, angled is weaker

Amphipathic = contains hydrophobic groups and hydrophilic groups 
Amphipathic lipids in water form spheres:
  -hydrophilic side on outside
  -hydrophobic side on inside

Colligative Properties: 
  -DOES NOT depend on solute, just concentration 
  -Boiling point, melting point, osmolarity
Non-colligative Properties: 
  -DOES depend on solute
  -Viscosity, surface tension, taste, color

Osmotic Pressure:
  -Water wants to equalize concentration along semi-permeable membrane
  -Hypertonic solution = more salt
    -Cell in hypertonic solution = Shrivel up
  -Hypotonic solution = less salt
    -Cell in hypertonic solution = Grow and burst

Buffers:
  -Made with weak acid and conjugate base
  -Resist pH change
  -Buffer Range = maximum effective range 
    -pKa ± 1
  -Buffer Capacity = How much a buffer can resist change
  -Enzyme catalyzed reactions have an optimal pH
  -Buffer systems in vivo based on:
    -Phosphate, mmol range
    -Bicarbonate, for blood plasma
    -Histidine, efficient buffer at neutral pH

Stereoisomers:
  -Two molecules with the same bonds but in different positions
  -Different physical properties 
  -Cis (Z) = Same molecules on same side of double bond (Largest molecules on same side if there are no two molecules that are the same)
  -Trans (E) = Same molecules on opposite side of double bond (Largest molecules on opposite side if there are no two molecules that are the same)
      -Cis and Trans molecules have different physical and chemical properties to each other
  -Chiral center = Carbon with 4 different groups attached
  -Enantiomer:
    -Same molecule with chiral center, but groups on chiral center are moved (Mirror images of each other)
    (See stereoisomers picture)
    -Same physical properties (Except polarized light) and different chemical properties
    -Same properties when reacting with an achiral reagent 
  -Diastereomers 
    -Molecules with at least two chiral centers but they are not all mirrored
    -Different physical and chemical properties
    (See stereoisomers picture)

Macromolecules have unique binding pockets:
  -Only certain molecules fit in well and can bind
  -Binding of chiral biomolecules is stereospecific
