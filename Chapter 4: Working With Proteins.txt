Aromatic amino acids absorb UV light, usually 280 nm (Tryptophan and Tyrosine absorb the most)
Coomassie Brilliant Blue G-250 binds to proteins and turns from red to blue

Column Chromatography:
  -Separate by charge = Ion Exchange:
    -Anion exchange = positively charged column
    -Cation exchange = negatively charged column
  -Separate by size = Gel Filtration: 
    -Smaller particles take longer to make its way through column
  -Separate by affinity = Affinity Chromatography:
    -Histidine tagged proteins bind to nickel
    -Specific ligands can bind certain proteins
  -Dialysis = Separate by osmosis
  -Electrophoresis:
    -Use electric field to separate proteins by shape and size
    -SDS-PAGE = sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis
    -SDS unfolds proteins and gives them a uniform negative charge, so they move according to their size only
    -Smaller proteins go farther
    -DTT = Dithiothreitol 

Average protein = 110 Daltons/amino acid

Orthogonal Techniques:
  -Using different properties to separate
  -Isoelectric focusing
    -Place sample on gel with pH gradient, proteins will separate according to their pI
    -Combine with SDS-PAGE to separate in 2 dimensions

Specific activity = activity / mg

Protein Sequencing:
  -Start at N Terminus
  -Edman's Degradation:
    -In high pH, add phenylisothiocyanate (PITC)
    -Lower pH, PITC will pull off first amino acid
    -Can repeat for first 40 amino acids
  -Sequencing:
    -Find N-terminus with FDNB
    -Use acid hydrolysis to determine amino acid content
    -Cut multiple samples with different protease to get smaller polypeptides
    -Figure out order of original protein by overlapping polypeptides
    -(See Sequencing Picture)

Sequences:
  -Identity:
    -25% similar = similar function
    -18-25% similar - similar structure or function
  -Homologs:
    -Similarities in sequence and function
    -Paralog = same species
    -Ortholog = different species

Protein Production:
  -Purification from tissues
  -Genetic Engineering (Plasmids):
    -The Lactose Operon:
      -LacI, Promoter, Operator, LacZ , LacY, LacA
      -LacI = Creates repressor that binds to operator, disabling protein production
      -Promoter = Spot where ribosome will latch on to plasmid to start protein production
      -Operator = Stop / Go signal, if repressor is bound to Operator, no protein is produced
      -LacZ = Codes for  β-Galactosidase, an enzyme that breaks lactose into glucose and galactose
      -LacY = Codes for an isomerase, an enyzme that will convert galactose into glucose
      -LacA = Codes for a permease, allows lactose to enter a cell
    -Selection:
      -After bacteria take up plasmid, add chosen antibiotic
      -Bacteria that took up plasmid will survive
    -Screening:
      -Add X-gal 
      -If bacteria turns blue, the lacZ gene was not destroyed and the gene for the protein of interest was not taken up
