Formula = Cn(H2O)n where n is a whole number
  -Polyhydroxyl aldehydes or ketones 
Produced from CO2 and H2O via photosynthesis in plants
Big range in size

Functions:
  -Energy source and storage
  -Structural component of cell walls and exoskeletons 
  -Informational molecule in cell-cell signaling 

Can be covalently linked with proteins or lipids to form glycoconjugates

Glycomics = Systematic characterization of all sugars in cells
Aldose = contains aldehyde group
Ketose = contains Ketone group

In sugars with many chiral centers, the farthest one from the carbonyl determines if it is D (right) or L (left)
  -Points with hydroxyl group
  -Glucose is normally D

Epimers:
  -Sugars that differ in only one carbon atom configuration
  -Diastereomers can differ in multiple places 
  -See Epimers of D-Glucose picture

Size Classes:
  -Monosaccharides:
    -Simple sugars
    -Most abundant in nature = D-glucose
  -Oligosaccharides:
    -Shortt chain of monosaccharides joined by glycosidic bonds
    -Most abundant = disaccharides like sucrose
  -Polysaccharides:
    -Polymers containing more than 20 monosaccharides
    -Can be linear like cellulose
    -Can be branched like glycogen

General Names:
  -4 carbons = tetroses
  -5 carbons = pentoses
  -6 carbons = hexoses
  -7 carbons = heptoses

Structures To Know:
  -Ribose = "Standard" five carbon sugar
  -Glucose = "Standard" six carbon sugar
  -Galactose = Epimer of glucose 
  -Mannose = Epimer of glucose
  -Fructose = Ketose form of glucose 
  -Glucose, glucopyranose
  -Lactose, sucrose
  -Glycogen, starch

Pentoses and Hexoses readily undergo intramolecular cyclization 
  -Last chiral OH attacks carbonyl 
  -Carbonyl carbon (C1) also known as anomeric carbon
