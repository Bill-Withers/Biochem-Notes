Amino Acids:
  -Over 300 amino acids
  -20(23) are used to build proteins:
    -Capacity to polymerize
    -Useful acid-base properties
    -Varied properties and functions
  -α-carbon:
    -Always 4 substituents: 
      -All (except proline) are connected to the carbon
    -Acidic Carboxyl group
    -Basic Amino group
    -Hydrogen
    -Unique R group
  -Zwitterion = Amino acid with protonated Amino group (NH3+) and deprotonated carboxyl group (COO-)
  -Amino acids can act as buffers in 2 ranges 
  -Proteins only contain L-amino acids:
    -L-molecules rotate polarized light to the left
    -D-molecules rotate polarized light to the right
  -All amino acids (except glycine) are chiral

Non-polar Aliphatic R-groups:
  -Stabilize proteins with hydrophobic interactions
  -Glycine:
    -High conformational flexibility (tight turn in structures)
    -Sidechain-less backbone (bind to phosphates)

  -Alanine:
    -Present in all non-critical contexts
    -Non-reactive
    -Substrate recognition or specificity (for other non-reactive atoms)

  -Proline:
    -Limited in conformations (Very tight turns)
    -Usually found on surface
    -Important roles in molecular recognition 
    -Binds aromatic residues on domains WW and SH3

  -Valine:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Non-reactive
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids

  -Leucine:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Prefers α-helix to β-strands
    -Non-reactive
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids

  -Isoleucine:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Non-reactive
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids

  -Methionine:
    -Slightly non-polar thioether group
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Almost always the first amino acid in a protein 
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids
    -Can be involved in metal binding

Polar, Uncharged R-Groups:
  -R-groups can form Hydrogen Bonds
  -Serine:
    -Conformationally versatile
    -Reactive OH group
    -Common in functional centers (for catalysis)
    -Catalytic triad found in many hydrolases
    -Site of phosphorylation
    
  -Threonine:
    -Conformationally versatile (Except for branching)
    -Reactive OH group
    -Common in functional center
    -Site of phosphorylation/glycosylation

  -Cysteine:
    -Outside of cell, frequently in disulfide bonds
    -Inside of cell, binds metals, like zinc
    -Reactive sulfur group, (Nucleophile)
    -Active sites, binding sites and metal binding

  -Selenocysteine:
    -21st amino acid
    -R-group = CH2SeH
    -Made by modifying Serine on tRNA molecule
    -More reactive and nucleophilic 
    -Active sites, binding sites and metal binding
    -Antioxidant defense and thyroid hormone regulation

  -Asparagine:
    -Surface of protein
    -Frequently involved in protein active or binding sites

  -Glutamine:
    -Surface of protein
    -Frequently involved in protein active or binding sites

Aromatic R-Groups:
  -Absorb Light at 280 nm
  -Phenylalanine:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Aromatic stacking
    -Non-reactive 
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids

  -Tyrosine:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Aromatic stacking
    -Reactive OH group
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids

  -Tryptophan:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Aromatic stacking
    -Nitrogen in aromatic system
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids
   
Positively Charged R-Group:
  -Lysine:
    -Prefers to be on surface of protein 
    -Hydrophobic side chain terminated with positive charge
    -Often involved in salt bridges
    -Binds nucleic acids
    -Site of post-translational modifications

  -Arginine:
    -Prefers to be on surface of protein 
    -Hydrophobic side chain terminated with positive charge
    -Often involved in salt bridges
    -Binds nucleic acids
    -Complex guanidinium group (Ideal for binding to negatively charged groups on phosphates)
 
  -Histidine:
    -pKa near physiological pH 
    -Relatively small shifts in pH change its average charge
    -Most common amino acids in active or binding sites
    -Common in metal binding sites
    -Charge relay systems (such as catalytic triads in proteases)
    
Negatively Charged R-Group:
  -Aspartate:
    -Prefers to be on surface of protein 
    -When buried in protein: salt-bridges (Arg)
    -Frequently involved in protein active or binding sites
    -Interact with positively charged atoms

  -Glutamate:
    -Prefers to be on surface of protein 
    -When buried in protein: salt-bridges (Arg)
    -Frequently involved in protein active or binding sites
    -Interact with positively charged atoms

Essential Amino Acids:
  -The Whole Foods Ladder Really Must Have Various Key Ingredients 
  -Threonine, Tryptophan, Phenylalanine, Leucine, Arginine, Methionine, Histidine, Valine, Lysine, Isoleucine

Peptides:
  -Small condensation products of amino acids
  -Peptide bond:
    -Resonance hybrid of 2 canonical structures
    -Resonance causes bonds to:
      -Be less reactive than esters
      -Very rigid, nearly planar
      -Have a large dipole moment, favoring trans configuration
    -A polypeptide is made of a series of planes linked at the α-carbon
    -Naming starts at the amino terminal end of the chain (Same for proteins)

Proteins:
  -In order to function, must be in native structure:
    -Defined by amino acids present in chain
  -Uncommon amino acids (Except Selenocysteine) are not incorporated by ribosomes:
    -Come from post-translational modifications
    -Reversible modifications, especially phosphorylation, are important in regulation and signaling

Main agents of Biological Function:
  -Catalysis:
    -Hexokinase (in the glycolytic pathway)
    -DNA polymerase (in DNA replication)
  -Transport:
    -Hemoglobin (transports O2 in the blood)
    -Lactose permease (transports lactose across the cell membrane)
  -Structure:
    -Collagen (connective tissue)
    -Keratin (hair, nails, feathers, horns)
  -Motion:
    -Myosin (muscle tissue)
    -Actin (muscle tissue, cell motility)

Proteins are made of polypeptides and possible:
  -Cofactors:
    -Functional non-amino acid component
    -Metal ions
  -Coenzymes:
    -Organic cofactors
    -ex: NAD+ in lactate dehydrogenase
  -Prosthetic groups:
    -Covalently attached cofactors
    -ex: Heme in myoglobin
