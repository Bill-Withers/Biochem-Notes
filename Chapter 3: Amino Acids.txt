Amino Acids:
  -Over 300 amino acids
  -20(23) are used to build proteins:
    -Capacity to polymerize
    -Useful acid-base properties
    -Varied properties and functions
  -α-carbon:
    -Always 4 substituents: 
      -All (except proline) are connected to the carbon
    -Acidic Carboxyl group
    -Basic Amino group
    -Hydrogen
    -Unique R group
  -Zwitterion = Amino acid with protonated Amino group (NH3+) and deprotonated carboxyl group (COO-)
  -Amino acids can act as buffers in 2 ranges 
  -Proteins only contain L-amino acids:
    -L-molecules rotate polarized light to the left
    -D-molecules rotate polarized light to the right
  -All amino acids (except glycine) are chiral

Non-polar Aliphatic R-groups:
  -Stabilize proteins with hydrophobic interactions
  -Glycine:
    -High conformational flexibility (tight turn in structures)
    -Sidechain-less backbone (bind to phosphates)

  -Alanine:
    -Present in all non-critical contexts
    -Non-reactive
    -Substrate recognition or specificity (for other non-reactive atoms)

  -Proline:
    -Limited in conformations (Very tight turns)
    -Usually found on surface
    -Important roles in molecular recognition 
    -Binds aromatic residues on domains WW and SH3

  -Valine:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Non-reactive
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids

  -Leucine:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Prefers α-helix to β-strands
    -Non-reactive
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids

  -Isoleucine:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Non-reactive
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids

  -Methionine:
    -Slightly non-polar thioether group
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Almost always the first amino acid in a protein 
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids
    -Can be involved in metal binding

Polar, Uncharged R-Groups:
  -R-groups can form Hydrogen Bonds
  -Serine:
    -Conformationally versatile
    -Reactive OH group
    -Common in functional centers (for catalysis)
    -Catalytic triad found in many hydrolases
    -Site of phosphorylation
    
  -Threonine:
    -Conformationally versatile (Except for branching)
    -Reactive OH group
    -Common in functional center
    -Site of phosphorylation/glycosylation

  -Cysteine:
    -Outside of cell, frequently in disulfide bonds
    -Inside of cell, binds metals, like zinc
    -Reactive sulfur group, (Nucleophile)
    -Active sites, binding sites and metal binding
  -Selenocysteine:
    -21st amino acid
    -R-group = CH2SeH
    -Made by modifying Serine on tRNA molecule
    -More reactive and nucleophilic 
    -Active sites, binding sites and metal binding
    -Antioxidant defense and thyroid hormone regulation
  -Asparagine:
    -Surface of protein
    -Frequently involved in protein active or binding sites
  -Glutamine:
    -Surface of protein
    -Frequently involved in protein active or binding sites

Aromatic R-Groups:
  -Absorb Light at 280 nm
  -Phenylalanine:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Aromatic Stacking
    -Non-reactive 
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids
  -Tyrosine:
    -Prefers to be in hydrophobic core
    -Aromatic Stacking
    -Reactive OH group
    -Recognizes hydrophobic amino acids and lipids
